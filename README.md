### In-Silico Screening and Molecular Docking of Medicinal Phytochemicals Against HER2 in Breast Cancer



#### **Project Overview**



This project explores the use of computational drug discovery approaches to identify medicinal phytochemicals that may have potential against HER2 (ERBB2), an important therapeutic target in breast cancer.



The study follows a step-by-step bioinformatics and molecular docking workflow, starting from disease and pathway analysis and ending with the prioritization of a promising phytochemical candidate.



#### **Objective**



To screen medicinal phytochemicals and identify a potential HER2-targeting candidate using:



* Pathway analysis
* Therapeutic target identification
* ADMET screening
* Toxicity prediction
* Molecular docking
* Protein–ligand interaction analysis



#### **Dataset**

* Initial medicinal phytochemicals collected: 76
* Compounds subjected to ADMET and ProTox analysis: 60
* Compounds selected for molecular docking: 12
* Final prioritized candidate: Rank 1 compound


#### **Databases and Tools**



##### Databases

1. KEGG — Pathway analysis and disease-associated molecular pathways
2. Therapeutic Target Database (TTD) — Therapeutic target identification
3. PubChem — Compound information and chemical structures
4. PubMed — Literature and research article retrieval
5. Protein Data Bank (PDB) — Three-dimensional protein structure retrieval



##### Computational Tools

1. SwissADME — ADMET and drug-likeness evaluation
2. ProTox — In-silico toxicity prediction
3. PyRx — Molecular docking
4. BIOVIA Discovery Studio Visualizer — Protein–ligand interaction analysis



#### **Main Analysis**



###### 1\. Disease and Pathway Analysis



Breast cancer-associated molecular pathways were investigated using the KEGG database to understand the molecular mechanisms involved in disease progression and to identify relevant therapeutic targets.



###### 2\. Therapeutic Target Identification



The Therapeutic Target Database (TTD) was used to investigate therapeutic targets associated with breast cancer. HER2 (ERBB2) was selected as the target for subsequent structure-based computational analysis.



###### 3\. Phytochemical Collection



Medicinal phytochemicals were collected from relevant literature and chemical databases. An initial set of 76 compounds was obtained, followed by preparation of the dataset for computational screening.



###### 4\. ADMET Screening



The 60 retained compounds were evaluated for physicochemical and pharmacokinetic properties using SwissADME. The compounds were compared using predefined screening and scoring criteria.



###### 5\. Toxicity Prediction



ProTox was used to predict the toxicity profiles of the 60 compounds. The predicted toxicity information was considered together with the ADMET results during compound prioritization.



###### 6\. Molecular Docking



The prioritized 12 compounds were subjected to molecular docking against the HER2 target using PyRx. The compounds were ranked according to their predicted binding affinities.



###### 7\. Protein–Ligand Interaction Analysis



The docked complexes were analyzed using BIOVIA Discovery Studio Visualizer to identify relevant protein–ligand interactions, including hydrogen bonds and other non-covalent interactions.



###### 8\. Final Compound Prioritization



The compounds were comparatively evaluated using their ADMET profiles, toxicity predictions, docking affinities, and protein–ligand interaction patterns. The Rank 1 compound was prioritized as the leading candidate for further investigation.



### **Results**



The computational workflow resulted in the following progression:



76 phytochemicals collected → 60 compounds screened → 12 compounds docked → Rank 1 compound prioritized

### **Limitations and Future Work**



* The findings of this study are based on computational predictions and therefore represent preliminary evidence. Experimental validation is required to confirm the biological activity, target binding, efficacy, and safety of the prioritized compound.
* Future studies may include molecular dynamics simulation, binding free-energy analysis, and experimental validation of the prioritized phytochemical.
